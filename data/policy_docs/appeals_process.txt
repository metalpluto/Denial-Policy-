Appeals Process Policy

Providers have 180 days from the date of a denial notice to submit a
first-level appeal, unless the payer contract specifies a different
window. An appeal must include a written statement explaining why the
denial should be reversed, along with any supporting clinical
documentation not already submitted with the original claim.

Appeals fall into two general categories: clinical appeals, which
dispute a payer's medical necessity or level-of-care determination and
are typically reviewed by a medical director, and administrative
appeals, which dispute non-clinical issues such as eligibility
determinations or timely filing decisions.

If a first-level appeal is denied, providers may generally request a
second-level appeal, and in many cases an independent external review
after internal appeals are exhausted. External review timelines and
eligibility vary by payer and by state regulation.

Appeals for eligibility-related denials should include proof of active
coverage on the date of service, such as a retroactive enrollment
confirmation or a corrected eligibility record from the payer, rather
than clinical documentation, since the dispute is about coverage status
rather than the appropriateness of the service itself.

All appeals should reference the original claim number, the specific
denial reason code being contested, and should be submitted through the
payer's designated appeals channel, which may differ from the standard
claims submission address.
